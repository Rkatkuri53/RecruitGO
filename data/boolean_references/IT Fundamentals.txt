Identify mandatory vs. optional skills.
Generate better Boolean strings.
Match candidates with much higher accuracy.
Speak confidently with hiring managers and technical candidates.

Common SDLC Models

Different organizations follow different SDLC approaches.

Model	Best For	Characteristics
Waterfall	Stable requirements	Sequential phases with limited changes
Agile	Evolving requirements	Iterative development with frequent feedback
Scrum	Agile teams	Time-boxed sprints (often 2–4 weeks)
Kanban	Continuous work	Focus on workflow and limiting work in progress
Spiral	High-risk projects	Repeated cycles emphasizing risk analysis
V-Model	Regulated industries	Each development phase has a corresponding testing phase
DevOps	Continuous delivery	Integrates development and operations with automation
Common Roles in the SDLC
SDLC Phase	Primary Roles
Planning	Project Manager, Product Manager, Program Manager
Requirements	Business Analyst, Product Owner
Analysis	Solution Architect, Technical Lead
Design	Architect, UI/UX Designer, Database Architect
Development	Software Engineers, Full Stack Developers
Testing	QA Engineers, Test Automation Engineers
Deployment	DevOps Engineers, Release Managers
Maintenance	Support Engineers, SREs, Developers
How This Helps You as a Technical Recruiter

When you understand SDLC, you can interpret job descriptions much more effectively. For example:

A Backend Developer primarily contributes during Development.
A QA Automation Engineer is most active during Testing.
A DevOps Engineer focuses on Deployment and Maintenance, while also supporting developers throughout the lifecycle.
A Business Analyst works heavily during Requirements and Analysis.
A Solution Architect is most involved in Analysis and Design, but often guides the project throughout the SDLC.
A Project Manager coordinates activities across every SDLC phase.

Recommendations for Your AI Recruitment Platform

Based on everything we've discussed over the past months, I would extend this beyond Boolean generation into a Recruitment Intelligence Engine composed of specialized agents:

Agent	Responsibility
JD Parser Agent	Parse and structure the job description into normalized fields.
Role Classification Agent	Identify role family, specialization, seniority, and domain.
Skill Knowledge Graph Agent	Categorize technologies, map dependencies, expand synonyms, and identify related skills.
Boolean Generator Agent	Produce platform-specific Boolean strings (LinkedIn, job boards, ATS, X-Ray) in multiple precision levels.
Candidate Matching Agent	Score resumes using weighted evidence rather than keyword counts, providing explanations for every score.
Gap Analysis Agent	Explain why a candidate is a partial match and identify missing critical skills versus transferable skills.
Interview Preparation Agent	Generate technical screening questions based on the mandatory skills extracted from the JD.
Recruiter Recommendation Agent	Suggest search strategies, alternative titles, adjacent technologies, target companies, and likely talent pools.
One Enhancement I'd Recommend

A lot of recruitment systems still treat skills as flat keywords. I recommend storing them as a graph instead of a list.

For example:

Spring Boot
      │
      ├── Requires → Java
      ├── Commonly Uses → REST API
      ├── Often Deployed With → Docker
      ├── Often Runs On → Kubernetes
      ├── Common Cloud Targets → AWS / Azure / GCP
      └── Common Build Tools → Maven / Gradle

This graph-based approach allows your AI to reason about related technologies, transferable experience, and inferred capabilities, resulting in much more accurate Boolean generation and candidate matching than simple keyword-based systems.

A world-class recruitment AI should not think in terms of skills first. It should think in terms of work.

People are hired to perform responsibilities, not to possess keywords.

Skills exist because they enable someone to perform specific responsibilities.

My Assessment of Your Platform Vision

Based on everything we've discussed over the past several months, I don't think you should build just a Boolean Generator or just a Resume Matcher.

The architecture you've been moving toward is much more powerful: a Recruitment Intelligence Platform with a shared knowledge graph and specialized AI agents.

One More Layer I'd Add

For a recruitment AI intended to operate at an expert level, I would add another component beyond recruitment knowledge:

Recruitment Decision Rules

This is where the AI learns how experienced recruiters prioritize trade-offs.

For example:

If a role is urgent contract hiring, prioritize exact skill match and immediate availability over long-term career progression.
If it's a permanent leadership role, place more weight on leadership scope, stability, stakeholder management, and strategic impact.
If sponsorship is unavailable, legal work authorization becomes a hard constraint regardless of technical strength.
If the hiring manager lists five mandatory skills but only two are truly business-critical, the AI should rank candidates based on the critical skills first rather than requiring a perfect keyword match.

These decision rules are what transform a recruitment knowledge base into an AI that reasons like an experienced recruiter rather than simply following checklists.

Example 1 — Notice Period
Definition

The notice period is the contractual amount of time an employee must serve with their current employer before joining a new employer.

Business Purpose

Allows the employer to:

Complete knowledge transfer.
Find a replacement.
Reduce business disruption.
Why It Matters

Hiring managers often need candidates within a specific timeframe.

Example:

Project starts in 2 weeks.

Candidate joins in 90 days.

Business impact:

Project delay.

When It Is Mandatory

Treat notice period as mandatory when:

The project has urgent delivery timelines.
The client explicitly requires immediate or early joining.
Contract positions require rapid onboarding.
Critical production support roles need immediate coverage.
When It Is Optional

Less important when:

Hiring for future projects.
Building a talent pipeline.
Graduate hiring.
Executive hiring with longer lead times.
When It Can Be Ignored

It can often be ignored:

During the initial sourcing stage.
When the client has not specified joining timelines.
If the hiring manager explicitly accepts long notice periods.
For niche skills where talent is scarce.
Impact on Candidate Ranking

Typical effect:

Immediate: Very High

15 Days: High

30 Days: Medium

60 Days: Medium-Low

90 Days: Low (unless the client accepts it)

The weighting should always be configurable.

Impact on Shortlisting

A technically strong candidate may be excluded from the first shortlist if the business requires immediate availability.

Impact on Boolean Generation

Normally:

None.

Boolean searches should almost never include notice period.

Instead:

Filter candidates after sourcing.

Impact on Candidate Matching

Notice period should never reduce technical match.

Instead:

Overall Hiring Fit

Impact on Interview Decision

If the client is willing to wait:

Interview.

Hold.

Very High.

Risk if Ignored

Possible risks:

Delayed project delivery.

Example 2 — Visa Sponsorship
Definition

Visa sponsorship means the employer agrees to support the legal process required for a candidate to work in the destination country.

Business Purpose

Enables international hiring while complying with immigration laws.

Why It Matters

A technically excellent candidate cannot be hired if legal work authorization cannot be obtained and the employer will not sponsor.

When It Is Mandatory

Mandatory when:

The role is in a country where the candidate lacks work authorization.
The employer does not hire without legal eligibility.
When It Is Optional

When:

The candidate already has unrestricted work rights.
The role is fully remote from the candidate's country (subject to employment laws).
When It Can Be Ignored

During:

Initial talent mapping.
Market research.
Pipeline creation before employer policies are confirmed.
Impact on Candidate Ranking

Should not affect technical ranking.

Should affect hiring feasibility.

Impact on Boolean Generation

None.

Never search:

Visa sponsorship

Instead:

Apply later as a recruitment filter.

Impact on Candidate Matching

Separate:

Technical Match

Legal Eligibility

Overall Hiring Fit

AI Decision Rules

If:

Requires Sponsorship

AND

Employer Doesn't Sponsor

↓

Mark

Not Eligible

Regardless of technical score.

Definition

The compensation the candidate expects for accepting the role.

Business Purpose

Ensures hiring is financially viable.

Why It Matters

A perfect technical match may still be unplaceable if expectations significantly exceed the approved budget.

When It Is Mandatory

When It Is Optional

During:

When It Can Be Ignored

Low.
